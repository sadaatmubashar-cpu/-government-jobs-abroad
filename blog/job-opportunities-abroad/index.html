Job Opportunities Abroad: Official Portals Guide 2026

Author: Hamid Aziz, site owner & editor About the author: Hamid Aziz is the owner and editor of Government Jobs Abroad. He is not an immigration adviser or a recruiter — every rule in this guide is taken from the official sources linked on the page, so you can check each one yourself before you apply. Published: 21 November 2026 · Last updated: 23 November 2026 Every portal below is an official government service, and applying on each one is free.

This page is the directory: almost every real job opportunity abroad is published on one of eight official government portals, and this guide explains each portal in turn — what it is, who it is for, and who it quietly excludes — so you can pick the right portal before you compare countries. (Comparing countries rather than portals? Read Jobs in Foreign Countries. Not sure what kind of foreign job you are even looking for? Start with Foreign Jobs Explained.)

The eight official portals, explained

USAJOBS — United States

USAJOBS.gov carries every US federal vacancy. It is also the most restricted portal on this list: almost all posts require US citizenship, and the federal government does not sponsor work visas for civil-service jobs. Treat it as citizens-only unless an announcement explicitly says otherwise. See the USA country guide and the latest snapshot in Latest USA jobs.

Job Bank and GC Jobs — Canada

Job Bank Canada is the national board where public-sector, health and education employers post vacancies — and the realistic door for foreign workers, because employers there can support work permits. GC Jobs is the federal public service portal, where preference goes by law to veterans, citizens and permanent residents. Read the Canada country guide.

Civil Service Jobs, NHS Jobs and Find a Job — United Kingdom

Civil Service Jobs covers central government (nationality rules apply — check each advert). NHS Jobs is the big international recruiter, with Health and Care Worker visa sponsorship. Find a Job covers the wider public sector, including sponsored school and council roles. Read the UK country guide.

EURES and EPSO — Europe

EURES links public employment services in 31 countries and is open to non-EU workers who secure a job offer plus a national work permit or EU Blue Card. EU Careers / EPSO recruits the EU institutions' own staff — EU citizens only, with two EU languages. Read the Europe country guide.

Choosing your portal: match it to your citizenship and field

  1. Start with eligibility, not vacancies. Run the eligibility checker for your country and field.
  2. Filter the 18 pathways by country, category and eligibility — open and sponsored routes are tagged in green and teal; citizens-only routes are tagged in red.
  3. Apply only on the official portal linked from the pathway card.
  4. Prepare in the employer's format using the application guides — federal resume, supporting statement, structured interview.

How to avoid job scams: the five-minute check

  • The vacancy cannot be found on the official portal? Walk away.
  • Anyone asks for a fee — for an application, an LMIA, a Certificate of Sponsorship, or a "guaranteed EU permit"? It is a scam.
  • The offer arrives by social media with no interview and pressure to pay today? Scam.
  • The email domain does not match the government or employer? Verify on the official site, not via the link they sent.
  • They ask to keep your passport? Never hand it over to an agent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do real job opportunities abroad start?

On official government portals. Almost every genuine route runs through one of eight portals: USAJOBS (USA), Job Bank and GC Jobs (Canada), Civil Service Jobs, NHS Jobs and Find a Job (UK), and EURES and EPSO (Europe). Applying on each one is free.

Which official portal is most open to foreign workers?

Job Bank Canada and NHS Jobs are the most realistic doors. Employers on Job Bank can support work permits, and NHS employers sponsor the Health and Care Worker visa. USAJOBS is the most restricted portal on the list: almost all US federal posts require US citizenship.

Do I need a paid agent to find a government job abroad?

No. Every official portal is free to search and apply on, so you never need a paid agent to “find” a government job. Anyone charging a fee for an application, an LMIA, a Certificate of Sponsorship or a “guaranteed EU permit” is running a scam.

How do I know whether a job opportunity abroad is a scam?

Run the five-minute check. If the vacancy cannot be found on the official portal, walk away. If anyone asks for a fee, if the offer arrives by social media with no interview and pressure to pay today, if the email domain does not match the government or employer, or if they ask to keep your passport — it is a scam.
