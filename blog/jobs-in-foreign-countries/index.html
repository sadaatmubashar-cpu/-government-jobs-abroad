Jobs in Foreign Countries: Which Countries Hire Foreigners?

Author: Hamid Aziz, site owner & editor About the author: Hamid Aziz is the owner and editor of Government Jobs Abroad. He is not an immigration adviser or a recruiter — every rule in this guide is taken from the official sources linked on the page, so you can check each one yourself before you apply. Published: 21 November 2026 · Last updated: 23 November 2026 Every portal named in this guide is an official government service; none of them charges you to apply.

This guide compares countries, not portals: which foreign countries actually hire foreign workers into government and public-sector jobs, what each country asks for, and where the door is closed to everyone but citizens. Canada is the most open, the UK hires into specific sponsored roles, Europe works country by country through EURES, and US federal jobs stay mostly citizens-only. (For the portal-by-portal directory instead, read Job Opportunities Abroad.)

Country by country: where foreigners can actually get hired

Canada — the most open door

Job Bank Canada is where public-sector, health and education employers post vacancies, and it is the realistic door for foreign workers, because employers there can support work permits. GC Jobs, the federal public service portal, gives preference by law to veterans, citizens and permanent residents. Read the Canada country guide and Jobs in Canada for Foreigners.

United Kingdom — sponsored roles, not open access

The UK hires foreigners into specific sponsored roles: NHS health work on the Health and Care Worker visa (care-worker roles closed to applicants from outside the UK in July 2025), teaching, and shortage occupations on the Skilled Worker route. NHS Jobs is the big international recruiter. Civil Service Jobs covers central government, but nationality rules apply to each advert. Read the UK country guide and Urgent Jobs in UK for Foreigners with Visa Sponsorship.

Europe — EURES, not EPSO

EURES links public employment services in 31 countries and is open to non-EU workers who secure a job offer plus a national work permit or EU Blue Card. EU Careers / EPSO, which recruits the EU institutions' own staff, is EU citizens only. Read the Europe country guide.

United States — mostly citizens-only

USAJOBS.gov lists all US federal vacancies in one place. Nearly every post is restricted to US citizens, and civil-service jobs come with no work-visa sponsorship — assume a vacancy is citizens-only unless its announcement names an exception. The USA country guide explains those exceptions, and Latest USA jobs shows the current snapshot.

What each country asks for before you apply

  1. Check eligibility first. Run the eligibility checker for your country and field before reading any vacancy.
  2. Match the route to the visa. A job offer is not a visa: Canada needs a work permit, the UK needs a Certificate of Sponsorship, Europe needs a national permit or Blue Card.
  3. Sort credentials and language early. Nursing, teaching and regulated trades need registration or recognition, and most routes need a language test.
  4. Never pay for the job itself. Visa and test fees go to the government or test provider — never to an agent who "found" you the job.

How to spot a fake overseas job offer

  • The vacancy cannot be found on the official portal? Walk away.
  • Anyone asks for a fee — for an application, a work permit, a Certificate of Sponsorship, or a "guaranteed visa"? It is a scam.
  • An offer that reaches you through social media, skips any interview and demands payment the same day? Not genuine.
  • A sender address that is not the government's or employer's real domain? Go to the official website yourself instead of following their link.
  • They ask to keep your passport? Never hand it over to an agent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which foreign countries actually hire foreign workers?

Canada is the most open: employers hiring through Job Bank can support work permits. The UK hires foreigners into specific sponsored roles — NHS and care work, teaching and shortage occupations. Europe's EURES portal links public employers in 31 countries for workers with a job offer plus a permit. US federal jobs on USAJOBS are mostly citizens-only.

What do I need before applying for a job in a foreign country?

Check eligibility first, match the route to the visa or permit, and sort credentials and language early. Regulated fields such as nursing and teaching need registration or recognition, and most routes need a language test. Visa and test fees go to the government or the test provider — never to an agent.

Is a job offer the same as a work visa?

No. A job offer is not a visa: Canada needs a work permit after the offer, the UK needs a Certificate of Sponsorship and then a visa, and Europe needs a national work permit or an EU Blue Card. The offer comes first; the legal route follows it.

How can I tell whether an overseas job offer is fake?

Treat the offer as fake when the vacancy is missing from the official portal, when payment is demanded for an application, a work permit, a Certificate of Sponsorship or a “guaranteed visa”, when it arrives through social media with no interview and same-day pressure to pay, or when an agent wants to hold your passport.
