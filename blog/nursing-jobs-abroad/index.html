Nursing Jobs Abroad: NHS, Canada & Europe Guide 2026

Author: Hamid Aziz, site owner & editor About the author: Hamid Aziz is the owner and editor of Government Jobs Abroad. He is not an immigration adviser or a recruiter — every rule in this guide is taken from the official sources linked on the page, so you can check each one yourself before you apply. Published: 21 November 2026 · Last updated: 23 November 2026 Sources checked against NHS Jobs, GOV.UK, Job Bank Canada and EURES — see the source notes at the end.

Nursing is the single most open government-linked route for international workers. Health systems in the UK, Canada and parts of Europe recruit nurses from abroad every year — but every route runs through professional registration first. This guide explains the three main routes, the order to do things in, and the warnings that protect you from scams.

Route 1: The UK NHS and the Health and Care Worker visa

The NHS is one of the world's largest employers of international nurses. Vacancies are listed on the official NHS Jobs portal, and eligible roles can sponsor the Health and Care Worker visa.

Registration comes first: the NMC

Before an NHS trust can hire you as a registered nurse, you must be on the register of the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC). For internationally educated nurses this normally means an English-language test (IELTS or OET at the level the NMC requires), a computer-based test, and later an OSCE practical exam in the UK. Start this process early — it takes months, not weeks.

How the visa sponsorship works

The trust must be a licensed sponsor. It issues a Certificate of Sponsorship, and you apply for the Health and Care Worker visa on GOV.UK. A genuine NHS employer will never ask you to pay for your sponsorship as a condition of the job, and NHS recruitment is always free for applicants.

See the matching route on this site: the UK NHS Healthcare pathway and the full UK country hub.

Route 2: Canada — provincial health employers via Job Bank

In Canada, nurses are hired by provincial health authorities and hospitals, and many of those vacancies appear on the official Job Bank Canada. Healthcare is a priority occupation, and employers in several provinces actively recruit internationally educated nurses.

Licensing is provincial

There is no single Canadian nursing licence. Internationally educated nurses start with the National Nursing Assessment Service (NNAS), which verifies your credentials and education for the provincial regulators; you then register with the nursing regulator of the province where you will work. Do not assume a licence from one province automatically transfers — check the destination province's regulator before you apply.

Work permit reality

You need legal work authorization: normally a genuine job offer first, then an employer-supported work permit. Never pay anyone for a job offer or an LMIA — charging workers for those is illegal in Canada.

Start here: the Canada Healthcare pathway and the Canada country hub. For the wider picture, read Jobs in Canada for Foreigners.

Route 3: Europe — hospitals recruiting through EURES

Public hospitals and care providers in countries such as Ireland, Germany and the Nordics recruit nurses internationally through EURES, the European Commission's job-mobility portal fed by public employment services in 31 countries.

Two things decide this route. First, qualification recognition: your nursing qualification must be recognised in the destination country, and the process can take months. Second, language: most roles require the local language at roughly B1–B2 level for patient safety. Many legitimate programmes include language training — a free EURES adviser in your target country can confirm the exact rules.

See the Europe Healthcare pathway and the Europe country hub.

The order that works: registration, then applications

  1. Pick one destination country and read its regulator's requirements in full.
  2. Start registration / credential assessment and your language test — this is the long pole.
  3. Search only on the official portal (NHS Jobs, Job Bank, EURES) while registration is in progress, and state your registration status honestly in applications.
  4. Verify the employer — licensed sponsor in the UK, a real health authority or hospital in Canada, a EURES-listed employer in Europe.
  5. Apply inside the official process only. No fees, no agents holding your passport, no "guaranteed" offers.

Warnings every nurse should know

Governments and the NHS do not sell jobs. Anyone asking you to pay for a Certificate of Sponsorship, an LMIA, or a "fast-track registration" is running a scam. Ethical NHS recruitment also follows the WHO code — employers should not actively recruit from countries on the WHO Health Workforce Support and Safeguards List, so be extra careful with anyone promising to bypass that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign nurses work for the NHS in the UK?

Yes. The NHS recruits international nurses through NHS Jobs, and eligible roles sponsor the Health and Care Worker visa. You must first register with the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC), which normally requires an English-language test (IELTS or OET), a computer-based test and an OSCE practical exam in the UK.

Do I need to register before applying for nursing jobs abroad?

Yes — registration comes first. In the UK you need NMC registration, in Canada you register with the nursing regulator of the province where you will work, and in Europe your qualification must be recognised in the destination country. Start registration early, because it takes months, not weeks.

Where are nursing jobs in Canada for foreigners listed?

On Job Bank Canada, the official federal job board. Provincial health authorities and hospitals post vacancies there, and employers in several provinces actively recruit internationally educated nurses. You still need provincial licensing and legal work authorization.

Do nursing jobs in Europe require the local language?

Usually yes. Most European nursing roles recruited through EURES require the local language at roughly B1–B2 level for patient safety, and your qualification must be recognised in the destination country. Some legitimate programmes include language training.

Is it safe to pay an agent to arrange a nursing job abroad?

No. Governments and the NHS never sell jobs, and NHS recruitment is free for applicants. Anyone asking you to pay for a Certificate of Sponsorship, an LMIA or “fast-track registration” is running a scam.
