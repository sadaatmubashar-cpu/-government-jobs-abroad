Government Jobs in the USA for Foreigners

The United States is the hardest of the four destinations on this site for foreign workers — and this hub says so plainly. Almost every federal job on USAJOBS legally requires US citizenship. What follows is the full picture: the rule, the one narrow exception, and the US-linked routes that genuinely hire non-citizens.

Author: Hamid Aziz, site owner & editor About the author: Hamid Aziz is the owner and editor of Government Jobs Abroad. He is not an immigration adviser or a recruiter — every rule in this guide is taken from the official sources linked on the page, so you can check each one yourself before you apply. Published: 23 November 2026 · Last updated: 23 November 2026 Sources checked against USAJOBS, USCIS and VA Careers — see the source notes at the end.

Do US federal jobs require US citizenship?

US federal civil-service jobs are filled under rules that require US citizenship for almost all posts. Permanent residents (green-card holders) are not eligible for most federal jobs, and the federal government does not sponsor work visas — there is no H-1B route into a civil-service post. US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) processes immigration applications for private employers; it cannot waive the citizenship rule for federal hiring.

Can a non-citizen be hired by the VA?

The Department of Veterans Affairs may appoint a non-citizen doctor, nurse or allied health professional when no qualified US citizen is available. It is a genuine but narrow exception, decided vacancy by vacancy — the announcement on USAJOBS states the citizenship requirement, and that line is final. See VA Careers and our nursing jobs abroad guide.

US-linked routes that hire non-citizens

  • Embassy locally employed staff. US embassies and consulates employ locally employed staff in the country where the mission sits, hired through the State Department's Electronic Recruitment Application (ERA) — US citizenship is not required.
  • International organisations. UN Careers, NATO and the World Bank recruit internationally by design, including for US-based duty stations.
  • Government contractors. Private companies delivering US government contracts hire under their own rules — see our guide to overseas government contract jobs.

Frequently asked questions about government jobs in the USA

Can a foreigner apply for US federal jobs on USAJOBS?
Almost all US federal jobs legally require US citizenship, so most USAJOBS postings are closed to foreign applicants. Permanent residents (green-card holders) are not eligible for most federal posts, and the federal government does not sponsor work visas — there is no H-1B route into a civil-service post.

What is the VA health exception for non-citizens?
The Department of Veterans Affairs may appoint a non-citizen doctor, nurse or allied health professional when no qualified US citizen is available. It is a genuine but narrow exception, decided vacancy by vacancy — the citizenship line in the USAJOBS announcement is final.

Can I work for a US embassy in my own country?
Yes. US embassies and consulates hire locally employed staff in the country where the mission sits, through the State Department's Electronic Recruitment Application (ERA), and US citizenship is not required.

Do I apply for US jobs on this website?
No. This site is a guide and takes no applications. Every application is made on USAJOBS.gov, the only official portal for US federal jobs, and applying there is free.
