Government jobs by country: how applying actually works

Each country runs its own system, its own citizenship rules and its own application format. Read your country’s guide before you write a single document.

USA

United States — USAJOBS.gov

The single official portal for US federal jobs. Over 100 agencies post here, and every application — resume, questionnaire and documents — is submitted inside USAJOBS.

How to apply for US federal jobs

  1. Create a USAJOBS account and complete your profile, including eligibility information.
  2. Build a federal resume with the USAJOBS resume builder — far more detailed than a private-sector CV, and capped at 2 pages (see the guide below).
  3. Search with filters for “Hiring paths” and check the citizenship line in each announcement before applying.
  4. Answer the occupational questionnaire honestly and completely — it is scored and decides who is referred.
  5. Submit before the closing date (US Eastern Time) and track status inside your USAJOBS account.

Official US portal

  • USAJOBS.gov ↗All US federal vacancies and applications.

Who can apply for US federal jobs

US citizens for almost all roles. US nationals and, in rare cases, non-citizens (notably some VA health roles) where the announcement allows it. Permanent residents (green-card holders) are not eligible for most federal jobs.

Visa reality check: The US federal government does not sponsor work visas (such as H-1B) for civil-service jobs. If you do not already hold US citizenship or an eligible status, federal jobs are closed to you — look at US government contractors or the other countries on this page instead. US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) processes immigration paperwork for private employers — it has no route into federal civil-service jobs and cannot waive the citizenship rule.

CANADA

Canada — Job Bank & GC Jobs

Two official systems: Job Bank (the national job board, including public-sector and health employers) and GC Jobs (federal public service). They work very differently for international applicants.

How to apply for jobs in Canada

  1. Start on Job Bank — search by occupation and province, and use the “international candidates” information on postings. Every occupation carries a National Occupational Classification (NOC) code — searching by your NOC code finds postings that keyword searches miss.
  2. Check your work authorization — do you need an employer-supported work permit (LMIA or Global Talent Stream)?
  3. Get credentials assessed if your occupation is regulated (nursing, teaching, engineering, trades).
  4. Apply to the employer exactly as the posting instructs; for GC Jobs, create an account and complete the screening questions in full sentences.
  5. Never pay for a job offer or LMIA — charging workers for these is illegal in Canada.

Official Canada portals

  • Job Bank Canada ↗National board — health, education, public and private employers.
  • GC Jobs (Canada.ca) ↗Federal public service jobs and student programs.

Who can apply for jobs in Canada

Job Bank: anyone may browse; hiring depends on your right to work in Canada or an employer’s willingness to support a permit. GC Jobs: preference by law to veterans, citizens and permanent residents.

Visa note: Canada is the most realistic of the four for international workers — but the route is normally job offer first, work permit second. Express Entry (permanent residence) is a separate points-based system run by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) on Canada.ca, not a job board — and provinces nominate their own candidates through the Provincial Nominee Program (PNP).

UK

United Kingdom — Civil Service Jobs, NHS Jobs & Find a Job

The UK splits its public hiring across three official services. The NHS is the largest international recruiter; the core Civil Service is the most nationality-restricted.

How to apply for UK public-sector jobs

  1. Pick the right portal — NHS Jobs for health, Civil Service Jobs for central government, Find a Job for the wider public sector.
  2. Read the nationality / right-to-work box on the advert before doing anything else.
  3. Write to the Success Profiles framework — UK civil service applications use behaviours and strengths, not a traditional cover letter.
  4. For sponsored roles, confirm the employer is a licensed sponsor — check it on the UK Home Office’s Register of Licensed Sponsors — and that the role meets the visa salary threshold or appears on the Immigration Salary List.
  5. Apply inside the portal and keep the advert — you will need its exact wording at interview.

Official UK portals

  • Civil Service Jobs ↗Central government departments and agencies.
  • NHS Jobs ↗Health roles across England, many with visa sponsorship.
  • Find a Job (DWP) ↗The government’s general job service, including public-sector employers.

Who can apply for UK jobs

Civil Service: depends on the post — UK, Commonwealth and EEA nationals are eligible for many non-reserved posts. NHS and schools: open worldwide where the employer sponsors a Health and Care Worker or Skilled Worker visa — for health and teaching roles; care-worker sponsorship from outside the UK closed in July 2025 (see the visa note below).

Visa note: Sponsorship is real in the UK — but only from employers on the UK Home Office’s official Register of Licensed Sponsors, and only for eligible occupations. A genuine sponsor will never ask you to pay for your Certificate of Sponsorship as a condition of the job. Care worker update: overseas recruitment of care workers and senior care workers (occupation codes 6135 and 6136) closed on 22 July 2025 — UK care providers can no longer sponsor new care workers from outside the UK. People already in the UK on that route may extend or switch under transitional rules until 22 July 2028. Nurses, doctors and allied health professionals remain open to overseas recruitment.

EUROPE

Europe — EURES & EPSO / EU Careers

Two different doors: EURES moves workers between (and into) 31 European countries through national public employment services; EPSO recruits the EU institutions’ own permanent staff.

How to apply for jobs in Europe

  1. Search EURES by country and occupation and create a EURES CV if you want employers to find you.
  2. Contact a EURES adviser in your target country — the advice is free and covers permits and qualification recognition.
  3. Start qualification recognition early for regulated professions (health, teaching, engineering).
  4. Secure the job offer first — for non-EU workers, the national work permit or EU Blue Card follows the offer.
  5. For EPSO, watch the EU Careers site for competition notices and prepare for the computer-based tests.

Official Europe portals

  • EURES Portal ↗Jobs and mobility advice across 31 European countries.
  • EU Careers / EPSO ↗Permanent and contract jobs in the EU institutions.

Who can apply for jobs in Europe

EURES: EU/EEA citizens move freely; non-EU citizens apply for jobs and then use the national permit or Blue Card route. EPSO: EU citizens only, with two EU languages.

Visa note: There is no single “European work visa”. Every member state runs its own permits (the EU Blue Card is the shared scheme for highly qualified workers). Anyone selling a generic “EU work permit” without a real job offer is running a scam. Two national schemes are worth knowing by name: Germany’s Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) lets qualified non-EU workers enter Germany for up to a year to look for a job, and Ireland’s Critical Skills Employment Permit targets shortage occupations there.

Application guides for government jobs abroad

Government hiring is a paperwork exercise with its own rules in every country. These three guides cover what actually decides whether an application survives screening.

01

The Federal Resume (USA — and the lesson for everywhere)

A US federal resume is a detailed work-history document that HR specialists score against the announcement — but USAJOBS now enforces a strict 2-page limit, so every line has to earn its place. Canada’s GC Jobs applications and UK Success Profiles follow the same principle: evidence, in the employer’s format, matched to the advert.

  • Respect the 2-page limit: USAJOBS caps resumes at 2 pages. Fit the detail in — cut older, irrelevant roles first, never the evidence.
  • Mirror the announcement: copy its duties and keywords into your experience — truthfully, and only where you have really done the work.
  • Add the details private CVs omit: hours per week, dates (month/year), and employer for each role in the last 10 years.
  • Quantify everything: team size, budget, caseload, systems used, results achieved — numbers are what survive the 2-page squeeze.
  • Never inflate eligibility: claiming citizenship or clearance you do not hold ends the application and can bar future ones.
02

The Cover Letter & Supporting Statement

Where a portal asks for a letter or statement (UK supporting statements, Canadian screening answers, EURES applications), it is scored against the person specification — point by point. A generic letter scores zero.

  • Work down the criteria list: one short paragraph per essential criterion, each with a concrete example.
  • Use the STAR shape: Situation, Task, Action, Result — with numbers in the Result.
  • Address eligibility up front: state your citizenship / visa position and, if relevant, that the role is one you are permitted to apply for.
  • Name the recognition status of your qualification if your profession is regulated.
  • Keep it to the stated limit: portals enforce word counts; going over can truncate your best evidence.
03

The Government Interview

Public-sector interviews are panel-based and scored against a published framework (US structured interviews, UK Success Profiles behaviours, Canadian merit criteria, EPSO competencies). The questions are predictable — preparation is the advantage.

  • Prepare 6–8 STAR stories mapped to the framework in the advert: leadership, delivery, teamwork, problem-solving, integrity.
  • Re-read the announcement: interview questions are built from its duties and competencies.
  • Expect an integrity question: government panels test ethics and handling of confidential information — answer plainly and honestly.
  • Ask about process, not perks: timelines, security vetting and start-date flexibility are safe, professional questions.
  • Vetting takes time: background checks and clearances can take weeks or months — a slow reply after interview is normal, not a rejection.

Frequently asked questions about government jobs abroad

Can foreigners really get government jobs in the USA?
Only in narrow cases. Almost all US federal jobs legally require US citizenship. The main exception is some healthcare hiring (notably the VA), where a non-citizen may be appointed when no qualified citizen is available. Green-card holders are not eligible for most federal posts. If you are not a US citizen, the realistic US routes are government contractors or state-level employers with their own rules — always check the announcement’s citizenship line on USAJOBS. Our guide to government jobs for foreigners without citizenship maps every open and closed route.

Which country is most realistic for an international worker?
For most people: Canada (Job Bank employer hiring with work-permit support, especially healthcare) and the UK (NHS sponsorship through the Health and Care Worker visa). In Europe, EURES routes into Ireland, Germany and the Nordics are realistic for healthcare and shortage occupations. The hardest are US federal jobs and EU institution (EPSO) jobs, both of which are citizenship-gated. See the country-by-country comparison in jobs in foreign countries.

Do I apply on this website?
No — and you should be suspicious of any site that says otherwise. This site is a guide. Every application is made on the official government portal linked from each pathway (USAJOBS.gov, Job Bank / GC Jobs, Civil Service Jobs, NHS Jobs, Find a Job, EURES, EU Careers). Those services are free.

Someone is offering me a government job abroad for a fee. Is it real?
Almost certainly not. Governments do not sell jobs, charge application fees through agents, or issue work permits without a real job offer and an official process. Never pay for an LMIA (Canada), a Certificate of Sponsorship (UK), or a “guaranteed” EU permit. Verify every offer by finding the same vacancy on the official portal.

Do I need my qualifications recognised before applying?
For regulated professions — nursing, medicine, teaching, engineering, most trades — yes, in practice. Recognition (for example NMC registration in the UK, provincial licensing in Canada, Anerkennung in Germany) can take months, and employers usually cannot hire you without it. Start recognition first, and state its status honestly in applications. The nursing jobs abroad guide walks through the NMC, Canadian and European recognition steps in order.

What is the EU Blue Card?
A work-and-residence permit for highly qualified non-EU workers, available in most EU member states. It requires a recognised degree (or qualifying experience in some countries), a concrete job offer in a skilled role, and a salary above the national threshold. Apply through the destination country’s official immigration authority — EURES advisers can explain the national rules for free.

Quick answer: can foreigners get government jobs abroad?

Yes — through specific routes, not general access. Canada and the UK are the most open to international workers; US federal jobs and EU institution (EPSO) posts are mostly limited to citizens.
